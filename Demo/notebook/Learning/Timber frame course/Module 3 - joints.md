# Module 3: joints

Evening course, week 7. Tutor is Owen. Workshop practice on 2026-11-24.

## Mortise and tenon

The workhorse. Tenon a third of the timber's thickness, shoulders square, and **draw-bore** it: offset the peg hole in the tenon by 2 to 3 mm towards the shoulder so the peg pulls the joint tight[^1].

- Haunch the tenon at the top of a post so it can't twist
- Leave the mortise a little deeper than the tenon is long, for glue and dust

## Scarf joints

For joining two timbers end to end when one isn't long enough. The stop-splayed scarf with under-squinted butts is the one Owen wants us to cut at the workshop.

> [!important] Length of the scarf
> At least three times the depth of the timber. A 150 mm beam needs a 450 mm scarf, and longer is better.

## Lap joints

- Half lap: quick, weak in tension, fine for a frame that's braced
- Dovetail lap: for a tie beam, it resists pulling apart

## Things I got wrong in the workshop

1. Marked the shoulders from the wrong face. Always mark from the **reference faces**, the two with the pencil loops.
2. Sawed on the line instead of beside it, so the tenon was loose.
3. Chopped the mortise before boring it out. Bore first, it's half the work.

See also [[Rafter sizing]] for this module's worksheet.

[^1]: Owen's rule: offset by the thickness of a 2p coin for oak, a little less for softwood.
